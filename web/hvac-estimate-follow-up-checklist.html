What to review in your estimate spreadsheet

  1. Keep a stable estimate ID. Preserve the original quote or estimate identifier so a review decision can be checked against the actual CRM or accounting record. Do not assume every spreadsheet row represents a different customer or job.
  2. Separate open from closed statuses. Distinguish pending/open from won, declined, cancelled and completed. If the status is ambiguous, hold the record and confirm it in the source system instead of inventing a category.
  3. Check estimate age and validity. Keep the original estimate date and any quote-expiration or validity field. An old quote may require a fresh price or availability check; never represent an expired quote as still binding.
  4. Respect the next-action date. If a salesperson has already scheduled a future action, keep that record separate from an immediate review queue. Avoid duplicate or contradictory follow-ups from different staff.
  5. Flag identical repeats. Exports can contain repeated estimate IDs or identical rows. Review them before treating them as additional opportunities. The same person can still have separate legitimate estimates.
  6. Check contact restrictions first. Review opt-outs, relevant communication permissions, prior complaints and the applicable contact rules for the location and channel. Missing information is a reason for human verification, not an invitation to message everyone.
  7. Assign a human owner and record the decision. For each unresolved estimate, document whether to review, hold, exclude, revisit later or investigate a duplicate. Keep a source reference and update the authoritative system only through an approved workflow.

What a useful review produces

A good review should produce a shortlist that a person can assess, an exception list for unclear or missing records, and a traceable reason for each decision. The goal is a trustworthy list—not an inflated count of possible revenue.
