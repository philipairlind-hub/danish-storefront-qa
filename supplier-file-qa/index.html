Supplier spreadsheet to Shopify CSV: a pre-import QA checklist

A supplier CSV or Excel sheet may contain 100 rows and still be unsuitable for Shopify. Map the data, separate exceptions, and review every source before allowing an import to create products.

This checklist is for ecommerce teams and agencies importing new products. It is educational content, not a live import validation service or a recommendation to overwrite existing store data.

Ten checks before your first product import

  1. Confirm that you are creating new products, not overwriting existing ones. Obtain the store owner's approval and compare proposed URL handles with existing products. Shopify's overwrite-with-matching-handles setting can replace existing values. Leave it off unless that is the expressly agreed operation.
  2. Identify each source column and its meaning. Ask the supplier what SKU, parent product ID, color, size, price, cost and stock fields actually represent. Do not guess a mapping just because column names look similar.
  3. Separate products from variant rows. One product offered in three sizes can require multiple variant rows. Confirm that product handles group the correct variants, and that the available option combinations are unambiguous.
  4. Check uniqueness, missingness and exceptions. Identify blank or duplicate SKUs, repeated product IDs, inconsistent handles, unknown option values and records with incomplete key fields. Hold uncertain records for manual review instead of silently fabricating information.
  5. Check prices and decimals. Determine supplier currency and whether a price is retail price or cost. Shopify's import fields expect the intended price structure; never silently apply an exchange rate or markup without an agreed business rule.
  6. Keep text encoding, quoting and delimiter rules correct. Confirm the supplier file's delimiter, UTF-8 handling, embedded commas, quotes and multiline descriptions. Shopify recommends UTF-8 CSV files; test representative rows before processing the full dataset.
  7. Validate publication status, tax and inventory intent. Decide whether new products should start as drafts; do not assume an unknown supplier stock number can be imported safely. Inventory across locations, product taxes and fulfillment rules need store-side decisions.
  8. Review image URLs separately. Shopify product CSVs use accessible image URLs rather than binary images embedded in the file. Verify image ownership, URLs and intended assignment; a file-only data service cannot promise to source or host images.
  9. Check dependencies before changing existing variant options. Shopify warns that option changes can create new variant IDs and affect third-party integrations. Test any update-to-existing workflow separately, outside a new-products-only service.
  10. Run a small authorized test and preserve a source audit trail. Review output rows, exceptions and source references. Test import in a development store or approved safe workflow first; keep the owner responsible for the final import.
