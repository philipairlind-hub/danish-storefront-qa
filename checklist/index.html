What to check before your Danish launch

  1. Verify that Danish is actually published and assigned to the right market. Open the real Danish-language URL in a fresh browser session. Shopify can display default-language content wherever a translation is missing, so do not assume that publishing a language translated every field.
  2. Check navigation and primary purchase buttons. Menu items, search, cart, discount entry, availability and add-to-cart calls to action should be correct Danish. A simple example: Læg i kurv is Danish; Lägg i varukorg is Swedish. Do not assume a scanner will recognize every contextual mistake.
  3. Search for untranslated theme keys and placeholders. Look for strings such as Translation missing: da..., English fallback text, template tokens and untranslated default theme content. Inspect product, collection, empty cart and error states, not only the homepage.
  4. Review money, separators and units. Confirm prices use the actual configured shop currency and local formatting. When displaying DKK, a typical Danish presentation is 199,95 kr.; do not convert currencies or invent amounts merely to make a page look Danish. Check tax and delivery wording separately.
  5. Check product-specific Danish wording. Inspect sizes, materials, units, variants, colors, care instructions and product claims. Prefer consistent terminology across collections and product pages. Do not silently change a legally or technically material specification.
  6. Check menus, filters, search and dynamic overlays. Cookie dialogs, predictive search, variant pickers, app popups, product recommendations and other JavaScript-rendered content may be invisible to a simple HTML text scan.
  7. Review cart and checkout in an approved test flow. Follow the Danish-language customer journey, including validation errors, shipping choices, address labels and order confirmation. Use test mode or authorized non-live test data; a public scanner does not test checkout.
  8. Preview transactional emails in Danish. Check order confirmations, shipping updates, account flows and returns notifications. Shopify supports language-specific notification preview; verify the actual templates used by your store.
  9. Review shipping and returns pages for understandable language. Confirm that customer-facing descriptions and links are present and clear. This checklist is not legal advice and does not certify compliance with Danish consumer or product-safety law.
  10. Check links, accessibility strings and page metadata. Verify translated headings, visible labels, alt text, page titles and links. Automated flags in HTML attributes may not be visible to shoppers, but still deserve inspection.
  11. Verify language URLs and international SEO behavior. Check the correct Danish page URL and canonical/alternate-language behavior. Shopify generally handles language URLs, hreflang and inclusion of published languages in sitemaps; confirm your actual setup rather than manually copying SEO tags blindly.
  12. Have a native Danish speaker read the most important pages. Assess natural tone, trust, clarity and whether the text sounds like a Danish shop. Machine translation and an automated scanner cannot establish this on their own.

Prioritize what to fix

Start with problems that block an order or materially misstate a product, price or delivery term. Then correct clear customer-facing errors in checkout and core product pages. Cosmetic style differences can follow after the purchase path is comprehensible.

A useful issue-log row: Page URL · element · current Danish text · problem · severity · proposed wording · owner decision. Keep the original text and mark changes requiring a merchant or legal decision instead of guessing.

Can an automated scanner replace native QA?

No. It can help identify text candidates on the publicly available HTML of one page. It cannot reliably judge meaning, voice, the checkout workflow, hidden content, customer-specific locale variations or the wording of every external app. A human reviewer should make final editorial decisions.
